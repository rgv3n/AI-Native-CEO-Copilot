# Margin Analysis — Expected Executive Output

## Executive answer

Gross margin deterioration is being driven primarily by **project execution leakage rather than weak revenue**. Revenue has continued to rise, but direct costs have increased faster, compressing gross margin from **36.0% in April to 27.5% in the September estimate**.

The largest current risks are concentrated in **P002 Orion EV Component Cell** and **P001 Helios Line 7 Automation**, with additional leakage in **P005 NovaGlass Furnace Controls** and **P007 Delta Water SCADA Retrofit**.

The strongest supported drivers are:

1. **Unbilled or commercially unresolved scope expansion**
2. **Overtime and rework on high-risk projects**
3. **Material / supplier cost pressure**
4. **Unfavorable portfolio mix, including structurally low-margin work**

**Confidence: High** that these factors explain a material portion of the decline. The dataset does not support assigning an exact percentage of the total margin loss to each driver without more detailed cost attribution.

---

## What changed

| Metric | April 2026 | August 2026 | September 2026 estimate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | €870k | €1.015M | €1.030M |
| Gross profit | €313k | €299k | €283k |
| Gross margin | 36.0% | 29.5% | 27.5% |
| Direct costs | €557k | €716k | €747k |

Revenue is increasing, while gross profit and gross-margin percentage are falling.

That pattern points toward deteriorating delivery economics and project mix rather than a top-line contraction.

## Cross-functional diagnosis

### Commercial leakage

Delivery teams are executing scope before full pricing or billing is secured.

This appears in multiple projects and should be treated as a process problem, not an isolated exception.

### Execution leakage

Overtime and rework are material on the same projects already suffering forecast-margin compression.

### Procurement pressure

Recent evidence confirms higher component prices and delays in critical supply categories.

### Portfolio mix

Low-margin projects amplify company-level margin compression even when revenue remains strong.

---

## What the CEO should do next

### Priority 1 — Resolve P002 commercially

Require immediate disposition of the **€88k unbilled scope change** and prevent additional engineering work without documented commercial treatment.

### Priority 2 — Put P001 and P002 on formal margin-recovery plans

Assign owners to:

- remaining procurement exposure,
- overtime containment,
- rework reduction,
- change-order recovery,
- and forecast-margin updates.

### Priority 3 — Review all active unbilled scope

P001, P002, P005 and P007 together show that scope governance is becoming systemic.

### Priority 4 — Review project acceptance thresholds

P007 suggests the company is accepting work with limited economic buffer. Contract-margin thresholds should be reviewed before additional low-margin backlog is added.

### Priority 5 — Separate external inflation from internal execution leakage

Supplier inflation, overtime and rework need different owners and corrective actions. Combining them into a generic "cost increase" category would hide accountability.

---

## Business risk if no action is taken

If current patterns continue:

- company gross margin may deteriorate further,
- EBITDA will remain under pressure despite revenue growth,
- unbilled work may convert into permanent margin loss,
- project delays may create additional overtime and rework,
- and cash pressure may worsen because some of the same scope leakage is not yet invoiced.
